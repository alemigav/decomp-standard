# PRESERVED_SYSTEMS — what must not be modified

A register of the reference project's systems that any adoption of this standard must leave alone, with the reason
in each case. Written as a constraint on future agents, including on whoever acts on
[`ROADMAP.md`](ROADMAP.md).

**The general rule, from the mission that produced this document:** do not damage the existing project — do not
delete scripts, do not rewrite verification infrastructure, do not rename established components, do not modify
original data, do not weaken gates, do not change expectations to obtain passing results, do not remove or rewrite
historical evidence, do not silently change the meaning of an existing metric.

Each entry below is a specific instance of that rule, and each carries what it would cost to violate it.

---

## 1. The gate registry and its expectations

**`tools/acceptance.py` `SUITES`** — 73 entries, each with a pinned expected case count.

**Do not:** lower a count, delete a suite, retarget a suite's command, or edit a fixture to satisfy a count.

**Why:** this list is the single definition consumed by three systems — the runner, the metric derivation
(`tools/counts.py`) and the drift baseline (`tools/ratchet.py`). Changing it in one place changes all three, and
the change is a one-line edit that does not look like cheating in a diff.

**Cost of violation:** the project loses the property that a correct count cannot be quietly lowered and a wrong
count cannot be baked in. The two guards are deliberately two-sided and neither is sufficient alone:
`acceptance.py` binds the count to reality, `ratchet.py` binds it to history. `ratchet.py --write` accepts
whatever the registry says, so it is worthless without `acceptance.py` having passed.

---

## 2. The mutation set

**`tools/audit_gates.py` `AUDITS`** — 146 entries.

**Do not:** delete an entry because its anchor broke; delete an entry because the mutation no longer reproduces;
replace an anchor with a weaker one to make it apply again; or reduce the judged criterion.

**Why:** the set is the demonstration that every gate can fail. A broken anchor is a `BROKEN` entry — reported, and
forcing `INCONCLUSIVE` — which is the correct outcome. Deleting it converts a known gap into an invisible one.

**Cost of violation:** the project reverts to the state where 49 of 132 mutations had never run and the number
146/146 looked perfect.

**Repair, if an anchor breaks, is allowed and expected:** re-point the anchor at the same mechanism, keeping it
unique and text-changing, then confirm the mutation still fails **for its intended reason** and not by exception.

---

## 3. The drift baseline

**`fixtures/gates/ratchet.json`** — `{suites: {...}, src_tree: <sha256>}`.

**Do not:** write it except via `tools/ratchet.py --write`; pass `--allow-decrease` without a recorded reason;
regenerate `src_tree` by hand.

**Why:** `--write` is a deliberate act that shows up as a diff. Hand-editing the baseline makes the ratchet defend
a number nobody accepted. `--allow-decrease` exists and should essentially never appear.

---

## 4. The decompiled corpus

**`src/`** — 3590 `.c` files and `INDEX.md`, hashed by the ratchet.

**Do not:** edit a body to make a check pass; edit a provenance header; rename a file; add a function to satisfy a
coverage figure.

**Why:** the ratchet's third invariant exists precisely for this. A verifier may add cases; it may not edit the
corpus to make them pass. `INDEX.md` is generated by `tools/export_src.py` and must equal the derivation.

**Permitted:** regenerating the whole tree from the journal via `tools/export_src.py`, which is how any legitimate
change happens, and which changes `src_tree` in a diff.

---

## 5. The published numbers in the documents

**`README.md`, `docs/handover.md`, `tools/ci.sh`, `.github/workflows/acceptance.yml`** — 20 quoting sites across
four files, gated by `tools/counts.py`.

**Do not:** edit a derived number by hand; delete a gated phrase; or relax a pattern.

**Why:** `counts.py` requires each phrase to appear an expected number of times, so **a deleted phrase is a
failure**. This caught a real edit: deleting `1022-case verification suite` correctly failed the gate.

**Note on the defect this register does *not* protect:** 15 figures in those same documents and in
`fixtures/sound/codec.json` are
**not** gated and contradict the registry ([`ARCHAEOLOGY.md`](ARCHAEOLOGY.md) §6). Fixing them is Phase 0.2 of the
roadmap and is *permitted* — it is repair, not weakening. What is not permitted is changing a gated number to
match a stale sentence.

---

## 6. The historical record

**`docs/log/` (229 notes), `docs/reverse_engineering_log.md`, `docs/MILESTONES.md`,
`docs/announcement-v1.0.md`, `docs/announcement-v1.1.md`.**

**Do not:** delete a note; delete a retraction row; edit a superseded claim to look correct; rewrite a milestone
row to remove an error; edit a published announcement to carry a corrected figure.

**Why:** the retraction table is the project's epistemic record, and the announcements are "left as published"
deliberately — a published document is a dated statement, not a live one.

**Specifically protected:** the retraction table's **header row** and its three section headings are literal
documentation anchors (`tools/check_markers.py`, 22 cases). Editing them fails the gate, on purpose.

**Permitted:** adding a row, adding a note, correcting a *current-state* document in a way that is visible, and
marking a superseded statement as superseded **in place** rather than deleting it — which is the project's own
practice ("`"Thread 7 never installs the vectors"` … is superseded").

---

## 7. The format laws and their validators

**`include/re2_formats.h`, `include/tyrant.h`, `tools/t64info.c`, `tools/t64_selftest.py`.**

**Do not:** change a constant without the measurement that changes it; add a field name that is not measured; turn
an `unknown_*` field into a named one without a discriminator; skip the corrupted-input half of the validator.

**Why:** these are the rebuild-facing codification of the laws. `tyrant.h` is generated from the registry and
gated by `--check`, so hand-editing it drifts from the measurement. `SCD_CTX_MEASURED` is deliberately a prefix
constant rather than a `sizeof`, because no `sizeof` was measured.

---

## 8. The committed artefacts and their single writers

Every file in `manifests/` and `fixtures/` has a writer and a `--check`.

**Do not:** hand-edit any manifest or fixture. Hand-editing is exactly what `export_ranges.py --check-images` was
added to prevent, after a fixture had been written by hand and had no producer.

**Why:** a hand-edited artefact is a claim with no derivation. The project's own rule: "a derived artefact is
regenerated from its source, never hand-patched."

**Permitted:** `--write` where the tool provides it, followed by `--check` and a diff.

---

## 9. The eleven cartridge-gated enforcement paths

**`tools/pre-commit.sh`** and the hook wiring.

**Do not:** use `--no-verify`; uninstall the hook; or convert a cartridge-gated suite to a skip.

**Why:** for 742 of 1028 cases, the hook **is** the enforcement. CI covers the other 286. Removing the hook is
indistinguishable from deleting the gated suites, and it is quieter.

---

## 10. The independence of the verifier

**`docs/VERIFICATION.md`** and the role it defines.

**Do not:** let whoever recovers structure also adjudicate it; let the verifier edit the corpus; let the verifier
lower an expected count.

**Why:** "make the suite pass" is a one-line edit in three different places, and the person best placed to make it
is the person who wants the claim. The document exists so the boundary is not renegotiated per task.

## 12. What adoption may change

Stated so this register is not read as "nothing may ever change":

- **Adding** a claim index, a schema, or a new check — with its mutation.
- **Repairing** a documented figure that contradicts the registry, provided the change is visible and the gated
  phrases are satisfied.
- **Adding** a cross-site quantity assertion (Phase 0.2) — this strengthens a gate and lowers no count.
- **Adding** `scope` / `does_not_say` / `state` fields as *new* records, leaving the prose notes authoritative.
- **Extracting** a mechanism into a separate module, provided the original tool's behaviour, output format and case
  count are unchanged and the full suite still passes with a clean admission.

Any change that lowers a count, deletes a suite, edits `src/`, removes a retraction, weakens an anchor, or makes a
gate's judged criterion less strict is **outside** what adoption permits, and should be refused rather than
negotiated.
